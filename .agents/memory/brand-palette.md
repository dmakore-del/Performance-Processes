# Brand direction

Keep the public website clean, minimal, and editorial. White is the dominant website colour. Use ink black for typography, fine rules, selected controls, and limited contrast moments. Use light blush pink sparingly as the accent. Typography uses a delicate but readable Newsreader display serif with Manrope for body and interface text.

**Why:** The user rejected the previous theme-like, WordPress-style presentation and explicitly chose a professional boutique-advisory feel with dainty, legible type.

**How to apply:** Use flat editorial compositions, sharp linear controls, generous but purposeful whitespace, and consistent reading widths. Keep most sections white or very light pink, reserving black backgrounds for rare intentional moments such as the footer or a media overlay. Avoid generic card grids, excessive rounded corners, pill buttons, duplicated utility strips, and decorative uppercase eyebrow/kicker labels.

All public photographs and videos must display in their original colour. Do not restore a global grayscale filter or alt-text-based colour exceptions. Curate media to create rhythm rather than filling every section.

Use high-quality professional media that reflects the company's work. People and faces in website photos and videos must look real, not AI-generated; prefer real photography and footage.

**Why:** The user explicitly requested professional, relevant graphics, video, and imagery, and rejected AI-looking faces.

**How to apply:** Use sourced real-world media for people, retain authentic team portraits, and never present stock models as actual staff or clients.

The homepage hero should feature a Black woman business adviser leading a diverse team through service-specific work. Photorealistic generated footage is permitted for this requested hero, but must not be described as recorded company footage.

**Why:** The user requested a longer service-showcase video with a Black woman lead and a diverse supporting team, and selected the resolution after the generated-video approach was explained.

**How to apply:** Show incorporation, consulting, digital systems, and funding work with natural movement. Keep this exception scoped to the hero, and retain authentic staff portraits elsewhere.

The company considers inclusion a core value. Photos, video, and people shown in graphics should represent different groups naturally across the public website. Visible labels such as “illustrative photo” or “stock footage” should not be added to the media.

**Why:** The user said the company is serious about inclusivity, asked for different groups to be represented, and asked to remove illustrative/stock labels.

**How to apply:** Use varied, professional work scenes without tokenizing people; keep alt text factual and descriptive, and reserve named staff profiles for authentic team portraits.

Keep blush restrained and never use low-contrast pink for small body text. Keep the pink logo and favicon. Preserve mobile-safe muted inline video playback. The user wants actual moving footage, not still photos with camera movement or static poster images; use an explicit play option for reduced-motion visitors instead.

Use commas rather than em dashes in prose, and prefer typographic structure over decorative icons, section numbers, arrow glyphs, and stock badges. Assign each photograph to one public placement only. Remove generic reassurance or pacing commentary; keep headings direct and supporting copy factual.

**Why:** The user has repeatedly asked to remove numbered section markers and decorative symbols, wants images to appear only once, and asked to remove generic website commentary.

**How to apply:** Keep headings direct, trim repeated helper copy, retain useful service details and instructions, keep navigation text-led, and check the public media inventory before assigning a photo.
